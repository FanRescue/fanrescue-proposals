Proposed Scope of Works

Below is the full scope of everything being supplied, installed, and commissioned at 380 Kenton Road. This is a complete system — extraction, odour control, and fresh air — with nothing left out of the price unless stated under Exclusions.

Extraction System

  • Supply and installation of approximately 6m of extraction canopy hood, 1250mm deep Fabricated in 304-grade stainless steel to cover the full cooking line. Includes grease baffles as standard. Exact canopy length confirmed on installation day — the 6m is the surveyed figure.
  • Supply and installation of approximately 2500 x 1250mm stainless steel canopy (secondary section) Second canopy section to complete the cooking area coverage, matching the primary hood in grade and specification.
  • Supply and installation of high-pressure centrifugal fan with inverter drive Specified to overcome the static pressure of the full duct run and odour control filtration, and sized to meet the council's noise conditions at the boundary. The inverter gives variable speed control — useful for balancing noise against extraction performance across different service periods.
  • Supply and installation of in-line acoustic silencer, fitted after the fan A key part of meeting the council's noise requirements. The silencer is fitted between the fan and the discharge point to attenuate fan noise before it reaches neighbouring properties.

Ductwork, Isolation & Access

  • Fabrication and installation of ductwork from canopy to discharge, with access panels throughout All ductwork fabricated in-house to the route confirmed on site. Access panels are installed at regular intervals — a TR19 requirement for inspection and ongoing cleaning. Without them, the system cannot receive a TR19 certificate and your insurance may be at risk.
  • Rubber anti-vibration flexible connections at canopy spigot and fan inlet/outlet Rubber flex joints isolate fan vibration and prevent it transmitting through the ductwork into the building structure. Directly supports the acoustic performance of the system and protects the integrity of the ductwork over time.
  • Anti-vibration mounts and rubber isolation for the fan The fan is mounted on rubber anti-vibration feet to further reduce structure-borne noise. Combined with the flexible connections and silencer, this is the full acoustic package needed to meet the council's noise condition.

Odour Control (Council Requirement)

  • High-efficiency odour control system, sized for the extraction rate and specified to meet the council's odour requirements A multi-stage filtration package designed to satisfy the legal and local authority requirements for commercial kitchen extraction. This is what prevents cooking odours from affecting neighbouring properties — and it's what the council will want to see evidence of in the post-installation report.
  • 2 x disposable pre-filters First stage of the odour control — catches the larger grease and particulate load before it reaches the primary filters, extending the life of the downstream equipment.
  • 1 x Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP) Electrostatic grease and smoke removal — ionises and captures fine grease particles the mechanical filters can't catch. A core requirement for compliant odour control in a residential or mixed-use area.
  • 1 x ozone odour neutralisation system Final stage — breaks down the organic odour compounds at a molecular level before discharge. Combined with the ESP and pre-filters, this is what meets the council's odour condition.
  • Full fitting kit for the odour control package All mounting, ductwork interfaces, fixings, and service access required to install the filtration train properly and leave it fully accessible for future servicing.

Fresh Air System

  • Full fresh air make-up system, sized to match the extraction rate Kitchens that only extract — without a matching supply — go into heavy negative pressure, which causes doors to slam, gas appliances to underperform, and uncontrolled air infiltration through every crack in the building. The fresh air system balances the kitchen, protects the cooking equipment, and keeps the space comfortable for staff.
  • Supply ductwork, grilles and diffusers Fresh air delivered into the kitchen in the right location and at the right velocity — not blasted into the canopy face (which would short-circuit the extraction) but distributed properly across the space.
  • Full fitting and ancillaries for the fresh air installation All supports, brackets, fixings, and interface components needed to complete the fresh air install as part of this contract.

Secondary Kitchen — Independent Extraction System

  • Supply and installation of a 3-metre canopy serving the secondary kitchen A separate canopy sized for the secondary kitchen, with its own capture rate independent of the main kitchen system. Complete with grease filters and integral lighting.
  • Dedicated high-pressure extraction fan, silencer and full ductwork run for the secondary kitchen This is a fully independent extraction system — its own fan, in-line acoustic silencer, ductwork from canopy to discharge, anti-vibration isolation and fitting kit. It runs separately from the main kitchen system so each kitchen can operate on its own.
  • Fan control, anti-vibration isolation and complete fitting kit to suit Speed control for the secondary fan, rubber anti-vibration flexible connections and mounts, plus all supports, fixings and ancillaries required to install and commission the secondary system as a standalone unit.

Commissioning, Handover & Council Documentation

  • Full system commissioning and airflow balancing Once installation is complete, we run the system up, verify extraction performance at the canopy face, balance the fresh air against the extract, and set the inverter to the operating point that meets the noise condition. The system is left working — not just installed.
  • Post-installation noise report for council submission A noise measurement report taken at the relevant boundary/receiver points with the system running at operating speed. Written up to a format suitable for submission to the council as evidence of compliance with the planning condition.
  • Full installation report for council submission A written record of the system as installed — equipment specifications, odour control arrangement, discharge location, commissioning results — formatted for direct submission to the council alongside the noise report.
